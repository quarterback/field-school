# East Detroit 2030: Department and partner role cards
**Public Mechanics | Student assignments and negotiation | V3**

Roles may be claimed individually or jointly. The class may create additional roles and split or combine work. **Internal** means the person or department must participate in East Detroit's planning process, but may challenge the plan and demand a responsible decision. **External** means the actor is independent and may refuse partnership. **Resident/community** participants can advocate or consent within their real or simulated rights; they cannot be presumed to speak for everyone.

These cards introduce realistic incentives and disagreements. They do **not** pre-decide any services, data exchanges, governance models, technology, contracts, or student voting outcomes.

## Internal city-county roles: expected to participate

### I-01 Mayor / County Executive's Office
**Authority to clarify:** Political agenda, charter interpretation and authority to direct administration; separate elected/legislative roles must be specified. **Wants:** a visible achievement without losing public confidence. **Worries:** concentrating authority, a failed public rollout, financial exposure. **Can commit:** administrative coordination within adopted rules. **Cannot promise:** consent from independent agencies. **Must ask:** Who approves a plan and who can stop it?

### I-02 City-County Council and Budget Committee
**Authority to clarify:** Appropriations, ordinances, hearings and public accountability. **Wants:** meaningful neighborhood representation. **Worries:** financial surprises, unequal service distribution, unchecked executive power. **Can commit:** votes only after lawful process. **Must ask:** Who pays recurring costs and who benefits?

### I-03 City Manager / Administration
**Wants:** clear delivery ownership and workable staffing. **Worries:** multiple systems with no operator. **Can commit:** coordination, staff assignments and performance tracking within authority. **Must ask:** Who operates this after the class finishes?

### I-04 Department of Human Services
**Wants:** dependable service for residents and defensible case decisions. **Worries:** student proposals confusing county administration with power to rewrite state/federal benefits. **Can commit:** process and local intake work subject to state rules. **Must ask:** Who makes eligibility and appeal decisions, especially when records conflict?

### I-05 Housing and Neighborhood Services
**Wants:** prevent displacement, reach renters and new households, coordinate with landlords. **Worries:** scarce vouchers, predatory screening, cost burdens and underfunded commitments. **Can commit:** municipal programs the council actually finances. **Must ask:** Which neighborhood conditions justify first priority?

### I-06 Planning, Building and Land Use
**Wants:** predictable permit rules, actual water capacity and protection of airport approaches. **Worries:** imaginary density approvals on restricted land. **Can commit:** local plan and zoning proposals for adoption. **Must ask:** What is the legal boundary and where may people safely live?

### I-07 Address Office and GIS
**Wants:** one accountable process for correcting parcels, service boundaries and addresses. **Worries:** errors from rapid subdivisions; postal, tax, E911 and school addresses differing. **Can commit:** accountable municipal GIS operations. **Must ask:** What happens while official records are disputed?

### I-08 Finance, Tax, Assessor and Procurement
**Wants:** consistent revenue assumptions, property assessment, procurement rights, transparent costs. **Worries:** unfunded operating obligations and commercial fees invented as law. **Can commit:** cost comparisons and buying pathways for approval. **Must ask:** What do we buy, own and pay for, and how can we leave?

### I-09 Digital Service and Records
**Wants:** maintainable services with accountable information use and accessible support. **Worries:** automatic joining of records, vendor lock-in, and assumptions that every resident uses a phone. **Can commit:** feasible architecture options and implementation tests. **Must ask:** Which system is needed for the chosen service, and whose authorization is required?

### I-10 Public Safety and Emergency Management
**Wants:** emergency location accuracy and resilient dispatch. **Worries:** private medical data and gate codes flowing without authority; poor coverage in remote areas. **Can commit:** dispatch and emergency service requirements. **Must ask:** What still works during an outage or partner refusal?

### I-11 Public Health
**Wants:** improve health access and surveillance of genuine public health threats with privacy protection. **Worries:** hospital independence, clinical confidentiality, unmet transport needs. **Can commit:** municipal population-health programs. **Must ask:** What information may be shared for what purpose?

### I-12 Parks, Libraries and Cultural Affairs
**Wants:** access across rich and poorer neighborhoods, service in person as well as online. **Worries:** facilities concentrated downtown, staff asked to become unpaid help-desk agents. **Can commit:** operating needs and program proposals for adopted budgets. **Must ask:** What is the right first service: recreation, library support, venues, or something else?

### I-13 Transportation and Public Works
**Wants:** safe roads, practical bus links, snow and storm response, access for shift workers and large events. **Worries:** confusing a fictional rail hub with agreement from RTD; unchecked roadway obligations. **Can commit:** city streets and locally authorized mobility programs. **Must ask:** What portion of the proposed network can East Detroit actually operate?

### I-14 City Clerk / Civil Rights / Appeals Officer
**Wants:** lawful process, access, public recordkeeping, protest routes, disability and language accommodation. **Worries:** unappealable automated decisions, decisions made without a recorded authority. **Can commit:** process standards and recordkeeping proposals. **Must ask:** Where does a person go when the service says no?

## External organizations: may negotiate or refuse

### E-01 Independent School District Board
**Status:** external even if its schools sit within East Detroit. **Wants:** appropriate capacity, enrollment accuracy, school transport and student privacy. **Worries:** the city's EDPS proposal preempting elected board authority, unfunded cooperation, and exposure of student data. **Can offer:** limited enrollment referral, address-check agreement, coordination funds, or nothing. **Possible refusal:** no access to student files and no automatic placement. **Negotiation point:** static referral vs shared processing vs independent case workflow.

### E-02 Regional Transportation District (RTD)
**Status:** external. **Wants:** reliable operations and fair service contracts. **Worries:** unapproved fare discounts, mobility credits, scheduling and station integrations. **Can offer:** public schedules, a formal service agreement, a tested pilot, or decline. **Possible refusal:** will not connect fare records to a city account. **Negotiation point:** what useful city service remains without an RTD API?

### E-03 Regional Water / Wastewater Provider and Special District
**Status:** external, possibly several districts. **Wants:** water reliability, capital contributions, enforceable capacity plans. **Worries:** approving housing density without secured supply; debt and rights transfer. **Can offer:** wholesale contract or phased capacity commitment. **Possible refusal:** no additional connections until infrastructure is funded. **Negotiation point:** land use sequencing and developer contributions.

### E-04 State of Colorado Human Services / State IT
**Status:** external program owner/oversight. **Wants:** compliance, data stewardship, benefit accuracy. **Worries:** altered SNAP/TANF eligibility rules, local duplication and unsanctioned state access. **Can offer:** sandbox, approved API, policy waiver only if authorized, or no integration. **Possible refusal:** no direct program data access. **Negotiation point:** local assistance workflow with formal appeal protections.

### E-05 Denver International Airport / Denver and FAA
**Status:** several external bodies with distinct powers. **Wants:** safe aviation operations, compatible development and control over airport lands. **Worries:** housing in approach zones, noise exposure and misrepresentation of legal jurisdiction. **Can offer:** public planning layers, coordination, or formal agreement where authorized. **Possible refusal:** no encroaching development. **Negotiation point:** preserve flight safety and redesign district boundaries.

### E-06 East Detroit Health System and Medical Partners
**Status:** independent health provider unless class establishes otherwise. **Wants:** emergency access and reliable referrals. **Worries:** clinical data-sharing and liability from automatic emergency tokens. **Can offer:** hotline, limited referral, emergency protocol, or no integration. **Possible refusal:** patient records stay in hospital systems. **Negotiation point:** when and how the municipality may request clinical information.

### E-07 Employers, Developers and Landowners
**Status:** private. **Wants:** clear entitlements, reliable freight and affordable workforce access. **Worries:** unannounced levies, long delays and water shortages. **Can offer:** contributions to mobility or infrastructure, phased development, or refuse proposed fee structure. **Negotiation point:** public benefits secured in exchange for specific commitments.

### E-08 Nearby Cities and Counties
**Status:** external. **Wants:** clear tax, boundary, drainage, service and road obligations. **Worries:** East Detroit shifting costs or annexing planned growth. **Can offer:** mutual aid, shared boundaries, transfer agreements, or refuse. **Negotiation point:** debts, workers, capital assets and service continuity.

## Resident and civic roles: give the plan a contested public

### C-01 Renter and Tenant Advocacy Council
**Status:** civic, not a municipal department. **Wants:** access to housing help, fair rent and clear appeals. **Worries:** public tools requiring tax records, exclusion from planning, automatic denials. **Can offer:** lived-experience cases and participation in governance if the city formally grants it. **Ask:** who is missing when a service is designed for a digitally confident homeowner?

### C-02 Neighborhood Alliance of Old Watkins and Agricultural Landholders
**Status:** civic. **Wants:** valid rural addresses, drainage protection, retention of businesses and farm-related history. **Worries:** displacement and a downtown-centric expansion. **Can offer:** local road and parcel knowledge. **Ask:** who bears land-conversion costs and who gets to appeal maps?

### C-03 Workers and Disability Access Coalition
**Status:** civic. **Wants:** service access for shift workers, residents with disabilities, people without a phone, and speakers of languages other than English. **Worries:** inaccessible online-only launches and unrealistic operating hours. **Can offer:** test cases and evaluation. **Ask:** what must work without a smartphone or same-day appointment?

## How to negotiate using the cards

Each role submits a factual or simulated basis, one main concern, a proposal or counteroffer, the resources it can actually commit, and the test it would require. A negotiated agreement should identify who signs, which specific powers it delegates, how to correct errors, and how to exit. The decision log records **yes**, **yes with conditions**, **no**, or **pending** without forcing an outside partner to agree. Internal roles cannot simply disappear from the process, but their objections and constraints should change the plan.
