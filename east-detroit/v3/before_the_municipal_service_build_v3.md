# Before the East Detroit municipal service build
**Public Mechanics | Service planning**

## Your starting assignment

You are a department head, agency leader, civic representative, or partner working in the City and County of East Detroit, Colorado, in 2030. The city has 382,417 residents, twenty neighborhoods, a large and unevenly developed geographic footprint, and major needs in housing, water, transit, benefits, address records, schools, safety, and community facilities.

Your assignment is to document services and responsibilities, write an implementation plan for shared digital services, and negotiate participation across agencies.

## Choose what you will own

**Claim a department or a specific decision.** You may also represent an independent school district, transit provider, utility, state agency, community group, or outside partner. Record your name and role in `municipal_service_signup_and_decision_record_v3.md`. More than one student may share an issue. A department head can bring several proposed decisions, and students may challenge one another's proposals.

Internal city-county departments are participating in the process because East Detroit has assigned them to do so. Leaders can contest details, budgets, feasibility, lawful authority, and sequence. Independent bodies can choose whether to participate and on what terms. Either outcome gives the class useful consequences to work through.

## Read before we meet

Start with the master dossier's city premise and the neighborhood atlas. Use the governance packet for options: Colorado and Michigan government rules, ownership and operation of shared assets, intergovernmental agreements, independent agencies, safeguards, and real-world comparisons. The map shows the county, its neighborhoods and the major transportation corridors.

## Do three pieces of work

### 1. Establish the basis

Name the community need or service problem your role sees. Identify the resident or worker who experiences it. Show which neighborhood or set of neighborhoods matters. Identify what East Detroit provides, what another agency controls, and where service information is missing. State the authority, money, staff, information, physical facilities, and partners your role would need.

### 2. Develop an implementation plan

Propose what to do first and why. State the policy decision, who can authorize it, how the work would operate, how it is paid for, what must be built or procured, and what users should observe. Name the alternative you considered and how you would test the result. Choose the level of technology and partnership you think is justified, based on what agencies and residents need.

### 3. Coordinate and negotiate

Identify which other department or outside organization must take part. Request consent or an agreement. If they decline, document their reason and revise the plan. If they participate only under conditions, record those conditions in the decision register. Make sure someone is responsible for complaints, correction, appeals, and ending an unsuccessful arrangement.

## What happens in class

Student owners present their proposals and challenge one another's assumptions. We compare conflicting requirements and decide what must be tested. The instructor builds or revises a working implementation model with AI in front of the class. The record preserves who proposed what, what the group chose, who dissented, and what changed when we tried it.

**You do not need unanimous agreement to make progress.** Keep disagreements, refusals, and missing information in the record. We can test more than one pathway where a decision remains contested.

## What you should arrive with

- Your role and service problem, including one specific resident case.
- The decision you propose and the authority on which it depends.
- A second option and its trade-off.
- At least one internal dependency and one external organization that might accept, negotiate, or refuse.
- What you expect to see if your plan operates, including a failure or exception case.
